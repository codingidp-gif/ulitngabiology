Systems of the Human Body: Transport & Defense

I. The Circulatory System

The body's primary transport network responsible for life-sustaining processes.

Functions

  • Transport: Delivers O₂ and nutrients.
  • Waste Removal: Clears CO₂ and toxins.
  • Protection: Carries immune cells.
  • Regulation: Distributes body heat.

Blood Components

  • Red Blood Cells: Oxygen carriers.
  • White Blood Cells: Infection fighters.
  • Platelets: Clotting agents.
  • Plasma: Liquid medium.

Blood Vessels

Type Direction Content
Arteries Away from Heart Oxygenated (except Pulmonary Artery)
Veins Towards Heart Deoxygenated (except Pulmonary Veins)
Capillaries Exchange Site Nutrient/Waste swap at cell level

Blood Flow Pathway (The Cycle)

1
Deoxygenated blood enters Right Atrium.
2
Flows into Right Ventricle.
3
Pumped to Lungs via Pulmonary Artery.
4
Lungs: CO₂ out, O₂ in.
5
Oxygenated blood returns to Left Atrium.
6
Flows into Left Ventricle.
7
Pumped to body via the Aorta.
8
Returns to heart via veins after dropping off O₂.

II. The Immune System

A complex defense network designed to recognize, attack, and remember pathogens.

Three Lines of Defense

1st Line: Barriers (Physical & Chemical)

Skin, mucus, stomach acid, and enzymes. Goal: Keep germs out.

2nd Line: Inflammatory Response

Phagocytes, redness, and swelling. Goal: Immediate non-specific attack.

3rd Line: Specific Immunity

Targeted defense using Antigens and Antibodies. Goal: Precision and Memory.

Key White Blood Cells

B-Cells

The "Arms Manufacturers." They create Antibodies to flag or neutralize specific pathogens. Some stay as memory cells.

T-Cells

Helper T: The "Generals" coordinating the response.
Killer T: The "Soldiers" directly destroying infected cells.

Phagocytes

Neutrophils: First responders.
Macrophages: "Big eaters" that digest pathogens and signal for help.
